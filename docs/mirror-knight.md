# Mirror knight

The non-Pokémon metal example is an original ornate armored figure facing left,
with a large convex silver shield and cyan luminous visor/gems. The broad shield
face is a separate drawing/material with an explicit reflective mask, distinct
from the brass border, armor and rough navy cloak.

- Package: `web/examples/mirror-knight.sprite`; thumbnail: `mirror-knight.png`.
- Native artwork: 109×159 pixels in a 192×192 canvas; ground anchor `[96,176]`.
- View: Front. Idle: 140 ticks, looping. Guard: 96 ticks, plays once and recovers.
  Clock: 60 ticks/second. Idle compresses the torso subtly from a fixed ground
  anchor; Guard leans/braces the whole figure and brightens the visor/gems.
- Parts: ground root, armor/cloth, mirror face and luminous lenses. Guard is a
  bracing pose change, not a hand-authored walking cycle or a separately moving arm.
- Shield roughness .065, metalness 1, reflectivity 1.5. Its authored convex
  normal field biases toward the left, where its scene counterpart stands.
  Armor uses metal/roughness maps; cloth has zero metalness and maximum roughness.
- All color pixels have binary alpha and belong to the saved 28-color palette.
  The native conversion uses 26 of those colors, with no dithering.

## Source and rebuild

The source was created using the **built-in image_gen tool**, then converted with
CIE Lab fixed-palette majority-cell sampling using the pixel-remake skill. It is
an image-generated original with authored material layers, not claimed as a
hand-drawn sprite or a reconstruction of game artwork.

`web/art-source/mirror-knight/generated.png` retains the unmodified generation;
`prompt.txt` is the exact prompt. `cropped.png`, `palette.json` and `native.png`
record the conversion inputs/output. The alpha-128 bounding box was
`(2,20,995,1473)` in the 1003×1568 source. The output width was 109 pixels, with
aspect ratio preserved. No geometric drawing substitute was used for the hero.

The native PNG and extracted layer PNGs are editable artwork. The readable
manifest is `tools/example_art/mirror-knight.json`; the `.sprite` also retains
all three color layers and their aligned normal/surface/emission maps.

```sh
python3 tools/make_mirror_knight.py
```

This deterministic step packages the saved native source; it does not call a
model or overwrite the other examples. The pixel conversion can be repeated with
the installed pixel-remake `tools/pixel.py quantize` using `palette.json`, width
109, maximum 28 colors, and `cropped.png` as input.

## Review

Inspected the native and enlarged silhouette, generated-to-native preservation,
shield mask and normal field, and Guard entry/hold/recovery. The actual native GPU
renderer accepted the package and produced original-color, material, normal and
foreground-depth snapshots under `web/art-source/mirror-knight/render/`.
`web/examples/mirror-knight-motion.png` shows the actual four Guard poses;
`mirror-knight-preview.gif` plays that clip. The drawing remains complete during
the small ground-anchored transformations. No opponent image is painted into the
shield; scene reflections are generated by the renderer.
